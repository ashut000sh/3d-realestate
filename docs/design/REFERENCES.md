# Design references

There are no reference images in the repo. These are the principles we borrow from four
well-known product families, written from what they consistently do. We borrow **principles,
never layouts**: nothing below should be copied screen for screen.

## Airbnb: listings and search

**What makes it good**
- Photography does the selling. The photo is the card; text under it is short and quiet.
- Price is always easy to find and always in the same place, in plain weight, not shouted.
- Search is two things side by side: a list and a map, linked. Hover one, the other responds.
- Filters stay out of the way (a bar or a sheet), so results start near the top of the page.

**What we borrow**
- Photo-first cards with nearly no chrome: no border box, no shadow, a 12px photo radius,
  two lines of text under it.
- One fixed spot for the price on cards and the listing page.
- Results first on Search: filters become a compact bar (and a sheet on phones), the
  list and the map share the page and highlight each other.

## Apple product pages: restraint

**What makes it good**
- One idea per section, said in one confident headline, with lots of air around it.
- Very few type sizes, used consistently. Hierarchy comes from size and space, not colour.
- Neutral surfaces; colour is rare and therefore meaningful.

**What we borrow**
- A strict type scale (six sizes) and big gaps *between* sections, tight gaps *inside* them.
- Each section of Home answers one question; nothing explains itself twice.
- Green means "do this", gold means "look at this number". Everything else is neutral.

## Sotheby's International Realty / Knight Frank: property pages

**What makes it good**
- Editorial: serif headlines, generous margins, large photography, almost no boxes.
- Thin hairline dividers separate facts instead of cards and shadows.
- Facts are set like a brochure (label above value, aligned columns), calm and premium.
- Very few calls to action per screen, each clearly the next step.

**What we borrow**
- Fraunces headlines with tight tracking; facts as a hairline-divided row, not four cards.
- The listing page reads like a brochure: photos, title, price, one primary action
  (Open 3D tour), one secondary (Book a viewing), and quiet text links for the rest.
- Illustrations, when needed, are fine line drawings in our palette (architect's sketches,
  not cartoon clip-art).

## Linear / Stripe dashboards: data first, quietly

**What makes it good**
- The first thing you see is what needs your attention, not vanity numbers.
- Dense but calm: hairline rules, small type, no decorative cards, lots of alignment.
- Charts have real axis labels and units, and they say one thing each.

**What we borrow**
- The admin dashboard opens on an action list ("2 scan requests waiting", "1 booking to
  review") and trends as labelled charts. No row of four identical icon-in-a-circle stat cards.
- My listings: each home is a row with a quiet stats line, actions on the right.
- Numbers in tabular figures, right-aligned in tables.
